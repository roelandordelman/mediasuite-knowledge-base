## What is this? *(Must)*

This document explains the scope, structure, and collection policy of the
Netherlands Institute for Sound and Vision (Beeld en Geluid / NISV) as defined
in its 2019 Collection Plan (*Collectieplan 2019*). It is relevant for
researchers who want to understand what is and is not in the Sound and Vision
archive, why certain materials are present or absent, how the collection is
organised, and what criteria govern access and acquisition. Understanding the
collection plan helps researchers interpret gaps in search results, assess the
representativeness of their corpus, and understand the institutional context
of the collections they are working with in the Media Suite.

## What it does *(Must)*

The Collection Plan 2019 defines the scope, profile, and development direction
of the Sound and Vision collection. Key things it establishes:

**Collection scope.** The collection covers Dutch audiovisual media from the
late nineteenth century to the present day: radio and television broadcasts,
documentary film, press photography, political cartoons and press illustrations,
newspapers and periodicals, oral history recordings, objects and memorabilia,
and — increasingly — digital-born media including websites, web video, podcasts,
and computer games. After the 2017 merger with the Dutch Press Museum
(*Persmuseum*), the collection now also includes a major press and journalism
archive with newspapers dating back to 1630 and over 40,000 political cartoons.

**Four collection pillars.** The 2019 plan reorganised the collection from a
format-based to a content-based structure, with four pillars and twenty domains:

1. **News, information and current affairs** — news broadcasts, political and
   social affairs, satire, education
2. **Culture and entertainment** — arts, music, religion, nature, animation,
   drama, sport, infotainment, games
3. **Amateur and corporate productions** — corporate films, advertising,
   amateur productions
4. **The media landscape** — production and distribution culture, media use
   and reception

**Statutory archive task.** Sound and Vision is the statutory archive for Dutch
Public Broadcasting (NPO) under the Media Act (*Mediawet*). This means all
public broadcaster radio and television output flows automatically into the
archive. Since 2012, approximately 8,000 hours of Dutch-produced television
per year enter the archive; since 2014, this includes over 50,000 hours of
radio annually. Twice a year a full week of Dutch radio and television — public
and commercial, including advertising — is recorded and archived.

**Archival services (tenants).** Beyond its own collection, Sound and Vision
provides archival services to other media organisations — storing, managing,
and providing access to their collections within Sound and Vision's digital
infrastructure. These tenant collections appear in the Media Suite alongside
the core collection but under their own access conditions.

**Active acquisition.** The plan commits to expanding the collection beyond
the public broadcaster into commercial, regional, and online media, and to
increasing diversity to ensure the collection reflects all of Dutch society,
including communities defined by background, culture, lifestyle, gender, and
ability.

## What it cannot do / known limitations *(Must)*

**Commercial broadcasting is significantly underrepresented.** When commercial
television arrived in the Netherlands from the late 1980s and commercial radio
in the 1990s, much of the entertainment, drama, and music programming produced
by commercial broadcasters was not archived. The Collection Plan acknowledges
this explicitly as a serious gap. Researchers studying popular culture, commercial
media, or entertainment from this period should be aware that their corpus may
be structurally incomplete.

**Online and born-digital media coverage is limited.** Web video selection
began in 2008 and media-related websites in 2015, but volumes remain modest
relative to the scale of online production. Social media content is not
systematically archived. Researchers studying digital culture, online video, or
social media discourse should treat the Media Suite as a partial source for
these domains.

**Access is collection-specific.** Not all materials in the collection are
accessible to all researchers. Public broadcaster material is available to
researchers with institutional SURFconext login; press and journalism collections
may have different access conditions; tenant collections have their own
agreements. The Media Suite shows metadata for restricted items but may not
allow playback or download.

**The collection reflects collection decisions, not objective completeness.**
What is in the archive reflects institutional priorities, acquisition strategies,
donor offers, and the statutory mandate at the time. Gaps in genres, periods,
regional coverage, and underrepresented communities are real and should be
factored into research design. The Collection Inspector in the Media Suite is
the primary tool for assessing coverage and completeness for specific
collections.

**The 2019 plan reflects the collection as it stood then.** The collection
continues to grow and policies may evolve. For the most current collection
information, consult the dataset descriptions in the Media Suite data register
or contact the Media Suite team.

## How it works *(Should)*

Sound and Vision operates as both a public archive and a media museum. Its
collection activities fall into two categories:

**Own collection:** Materials acquired, licensed, or received by Sound and Vision
for its own collection, stored and preserved in its digital archive (currently
approximately seventeen petabytes). This includes the statutory public broadcaster
archive, active and passive acquisition from media companies and private donors,
and digital-born selection.

**Tenant collections:** Collections managed by Sound and Vision on behalf of
other organisations (media companies, cultural institutions, research
organisations). These organisations retain rights over their collections while
benefiting from Sound and Vision's preservation and access infrastructure. Tenant
collections may appear in the Media Suite under their own access conditions.

Collection development follows domain-specific selection documents for each of
the twenty domains, which define selection criteria, collection goals, and known
gaps. These documents are updated periodically.

## Where the information comes from *(Should)*

The Sound and Vision collection originated from the 1997 merger of four
predecessor institutions: the audiovisual archive of Dutch public broadcasting
(AVAC, which included the Polygoon-Profilti film archive and oral history
collections), the film archive of the Government Information Service (RVD,
including material from the late nineteenth century), the Foundation Film and
Science (*Stichting Film en Wetenschap*), and the Broadcasting Museum
(*Omroepmuseum*). The 2017 merger with the Dutch Press Museum added the
press and journalism collection.

Major digitisation initiatives — particularly *Beelden voor de Toekomst*
(Images for the Future, 2007–2014) — transformed access to the historical
collection. Ongoing digital ingest of public broadcaster content provides
continuous growth.

The Collection Plan itself is a public document produced by Sound and Vision
in 2019, available via the Sound and Vision website. It is reviewed and updated
periodically.

## Design decisions and rationale *(Could)*

The shift from format-based to content-based collection domains in the 2019
plan reflects the convergence of media forms in the digital era: a political
interview might exist as a television broadcast, a web video, a podcast, and a
social media clip simultaneously. Organising by content domain rather than
carrier or format makes the collection more coherent for researchers approaching
it thematically rather than by medium.

The explicit commitment to diversity in the 2019 plan — ensuring the collection
is recognisable and relevant to all Dutch residents — reflects both a social
mandate and an acknowledgment that historical collecting priorities have
underrepresented certain communities and perspectives. Researchers working on
topics involving minority communities, regional culture, or underrepresented
groups should be aware that the collection may reflect these historical biases.

Sound and Vision's position in the NDE (Netwerk Digitaal Erfgoed / Digital
Heritage Network) and its collaboration with the KB, National Archives, Eye
Filmmuseum, and other institutions shapes what Sound and Vision collects and
what other institutions collect — with coordination to avoid duplication and
to fill gaps across the ecosystem.
